# 1. Summary and decision points

Public hospital and community characteristics describe a small share of the variation in published PSI-90 within the analyzed sample. Community associations change across geographic constructions and model choices, which limits how confidently the score can inform community adjustment or data prioritization. The payment scenarios identify assumptions that need validation before they can guide incentive design. These are the answers to whether CMS's PSI-90, which HACRP penalizes on and a Q-HIP-style bonus would reward, follows the conditions of the communities hospitals serve; because the analysis observes neither safety effort nor how scores respond to investment, each is an association across hospitals, and none separates a shortfall in risk adjustment from a real difference in care.

Across the 2,569 hospitals with every covariate observed, the community block is associated with 3.3% of the variation in published PSI-90 (95% bootstrap interval 2.5% to 4.8%) and hospital structure with 4.5% (3.5% to 6.8%); in a separate model of the four component indicators across all 2,801 hospitals, community accounts for 1.44% of the between-hospital variance (bootstrap interval over hospital service areas, −0.60% to 3.67%), an interval that includes zero. The 91.2% left unexplained (bootstrap interval 87.9% to 92.6%) is conditional on these covariates and this sample, and it is not a measure of hospital effort. Community adjustment of the score could therefore change it only modestly on average, although near a payment cut point a small change can move a hospital across it.

The direction of the association is unsettled. Every one of the 194 near-optimal models fitted to those hospitals relies on the disadvantage measures at the primary tolerance (model class reliance of at least 1.085, a lower bound that falls to 1.000 at the state tolerance), but their estimates of how predicted PSI-90 differs between catchments at the 10th and 90th percentiles of disadvantage range from −0.37 to +0.86 standard deviations, a range across models rather than a sampling interval. The answer also moves with how catchment measures are built, and filling suppressed discharge counts reorders individual hospitals' community contributions (rank correlation 0.840 among the 251 hospitals with the fewest mapped discharges, in a fill scenario with no probability attached), so public data do not support a hospital-by-hospital adjustment.

The community measures still earn a place out of sample. For the same hospitals, scored in states the model has not seen, dropping them raises held-out error by 2.2% (1.0% to 3.1% over 20 repeated fold assignments), and the four SVI themes with the limited-English share come within 0.37% of the full block (−0.98% to 1.34%) while ADI alone falls 1.30% short (0.11% to 2.25%). The base is small: the best model explains 4.0% of the variation in unseen states, a share for which no interval was computed.

HACRP, which scores PSI-90 beside five infection measures, already penalizes unevenly. In FY 2026, among the 2,551 hospitals with a HACRP decision, those in the most deprived fifth of catchments were penalized at 1.39 times the rate of the least deprived fifth (bootstrap interval 1.14 to 1.71). Q-HIP's formula is not public, so the simulation applies a Q-HIP-style rule as CASH2 specified it to the 2,569 hospitals. Being rank-based, that rule pays $0 more if every hospital improves at once, by construction and with no modeled uncertainty. Its return to improvement per revenue dollar falls with catchment deprivation (concentration index −0.058, 95% simulation interval −0.073 to −0.044), mainly through the commercial share of revenue, and loses its direction under CASH2's 0.40 safety-net revenue factor. Along the models' own disadvantage ranking the result is on the edge (−0.038, simulation interval −0.085 to −0.0004).

Four decisions follow for CASH. The first is not to adjust PSI-90 for community conditions hospital by hospital on public data alone, since the direction is unsettled and individual contributions are unstable. The second is that a community-data module for safety-score analysis, if CASH keeps one, should start from the SVI themes with the limited-English share rather than ADI alone, as a module for this analysis and not a minimum dataset for a CASH2 business case. The third is that any incentive design should be judged on its revenue base, which moved the concentration more than any other assumption the scenarios varied, and on whether it credits year-over-year improvement, which a rank-based rule cannot reward and this simulation did not model. The fourth is which question comes next, fairness with patient-level data or return on investment with hospital-level inputs, knowing that the CASH2 Tracks do not correspond to stable groups in the national file.
