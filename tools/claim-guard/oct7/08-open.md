# 8. What remains open

The largest open question is the one the design cannot reach. A single cross-section of published scores cannot tell a shortfall in risk adjustment from a real difference in the care that hospitals in disadvantaged catchments provide, and the report's fairness findings are bounded by that: they say how much of PSI-90's variation public community measures describe and how unsettled the direction of the association is, not whether the score is unfair to any hospital. Answering the stronger question would need patient-level data, such as the HCUP state inpatient databases, in which risk adjustment can be examined directly, or a panel of reporting windows in which a hospital's own changes can be followed.

The reliability used in the weighted sensitivity analyses rests on a scalar approximation to CMS's interval construction. The AHRQ documentation for the software CMS uses indicates that the signal covariances among the components are positive, which would make the approximation's reliability too low, and a question to the QualityNet help desk asking how the FY 2026 intervals were built has been drafted. Its answer bears on the sensitivity analyses only, since every primary result uses the published score unweighted. A related limit appears in the payment check: in the quarter of hospitals with the lowest reliability, the calibration slope against observed HACRP penalties is 1.29, outside the bar of 0.8 to 1.2 set in advance, so the simulated scores are least dependable where the published scores are least reliable.

The CASH2 Tracks do not correspond to stable groups in the national data. On star rating and operating margin, 1,591 of 2,581 hospitals (61.6%) fall outside the three cells the Tracks describe, and on PSI-90 and operating margin across 2,759 hospitals, ICL selects 2 groups on the published score and 4 on the reliability-corrected score while the gap statistic selects 1 on both, with ICL's own choice recurring in only 44% and 59% of 100 random 80% subsamples. The analysis therefore does not establish stable three-Track groups, and an ROI exercise built on the national file should use hospital-level inputs rather than per-Track parameters.

The direct and shared reliance method that the earlier draft used to rank individual community measures was repaired in the second pass, and after the repair it tags no measure as essential or substitutable once the metropolitan-area tolerance is applied; at the primary tolerance only the limited-English share's unique part clears the threshold, and narrowly. It is reported in the technical supplement as exploratory, with its lineage, and no recommendation in this report rests on it; the candidate community-data module rests on the held-out validation in section 6 instead.

Four pieces of work are deferred past the contract. The payment simulation models rewards for rank but not credit for year-over-year improvement, which Elevance describes as part of its program, and modeling that credit needs a second PSI-90 reporting window. The value of HCUP state inpatient data, Leapfrog grades and a panel dataset depends on access and on which of the questions above CASH wants answered first. The cross-term share of the shared-reliance allocation is uncomputed, and matters only if the supplement keeps that method's flow table. And the complete-case restriction, which section 3 identifies as the missing-data assumption that matters, could be tested with an imputation model for operating margin, uncompensated care and market concentration and a refit of the 736 composite specifications.

The return-on-investment question is the next exercise rather than part of this report. Its definition, kept with the CASH2 revision ledger, lists the inputs a hospital-level ROI needs (eligible revenue, intervention cost, achievable reduction and payment timing among them) with a source for each, and leaves open whether the unit should be the facility or the system. Settling that unit, and deciding whether the deliverable should next serve the fairness question with patient-level data or the investment question with hospital-level inputs, are the decisions this report leaves to CASH.
