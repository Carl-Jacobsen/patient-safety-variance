# 4. Methods

Many models fit these data about equally well without giving the same answer about community conditions, so the methods report the range of answers among well-fitting models rather than the answer of one preferred model. The outcome in every primary analysis is the published PSI-90 score, unweighted, because that is the number CMS reports and pays on; fits that weight hospitals by their reliability are run as sensitivity analyses, because the reliability they use rests on a scalar approximation that is likely biased low.

The first method divides the variation in PSI-90 among the three covariate blocks. Adding blocks one at a time credits whichever block enters first with the variation the blocks share, so the decomposition averages each block's contribution over every order of entry (a procedure known as LMG or Shapley averaging), and its intervals come from refitting it on 1,000 resampled versions of the data. The same decomposition is run on the four component indicators, which CMS publishes without composite smoothing, as a check on a different target.

The second method maps the choices an analyst could reasonably make: which blocks to include, whether relationships are straight lines or curves, which outcome to model, how to treat reliability, at what geographic grain to measure the community, how to handle missing values, whether to set aside unusual hospitals, and which scale to use. Every combination of those choices was registered before any model was fitted, which gives 3,496 specifications, 736 of them for the composite score. Each specification that includes the community block yields a contrast: the difference in predicted PSI-90, in standard deviations, between a catchment at the 10th percentile of every disadvantage measure and one at the 90th.

The third method narrows those specifications to the ones that fit nearly as well as the best. This group of near-optimal models is called a Rashomon set, after the film in which witnesses give equally plausible but conflicting accounts of one event. A model belongs to the set if its prediction error exceeds the best model's by no more than a tolerance, and the tolerance was set by procedure rather than judgment: the best model was refitted on 1,000 resampled versions of the data, and the tolerance is the 95th percentile of the spread in their errors, fixed in writing before any error was computed. Resampling individual hospitals gives a tolerance of 4.1% of the best model's error and admits 194 of the 736 composite specifications. Resampling whole metropolitan areas gives 5.1%, and resampling states gives 9.3%, which treats hospitals in the same area as sharing unmeasured influences; the hospital tolerance is primary because it was fixed in advance, and results are reported at all three.

The fourth method measures how much the models in the set depend on the community measures. A model's reliance on a group of covariates is the ratio of its prediction error after their values are scrambled across hospitals to its error before, so a ratio of 1 means the model does no worse without them. Model class reliance (MCR) reports the lowest and highest reliance across every model in the Rashomon set. A lowest value above 1 says that none of the near-optimal models, among those enumerated, can ignore the group without losing accuracy; it says nothing about the direction of the association, it describes the models rather than the world, and it holds only for the 736 specifications listed and only at the tolerance stated.

The fifth method tests whether the community measures earn their place outside the data they were fitted to. The best model was refitted with four versions of the community block (none, all of it, ADI alone, and the four SVI themes with the limited-English share), and each version was scored on hospitals it had not seen, in two designs: folds made of whole states, which asks how well the model predicts in new areas, and folds of hospitals within known areas. Each design was repeated over 20 random fold assignments, and a reduced version counts as equivalent to the full block if its held-out error is within 1% of the full block's. This validation was added after the external review and is labeled as such.

The sixth method simulates payment. Q-HIP's formula is not public, so the simulation applies a Q-HIP-style rule as CASH2 specified it: a bonus of 0.1% to 4% of each hospital's commercial revenue, set by the hospital's rank on a safety score in which PSI-90 carries a fixed weight beside the HACRP infection measures. Commercial revenue is each hospital's net patient revenue times its commercial share, estimated from its cost report. The quantity of interest is the finite payment return, the change in a hospital's bonus if its PSI-90 improves by a fixed amount while every other hospital's score stays where it is, divided by its revenue so that hospitals of different size can be compared. Its concentration index summarizes whether the return falls more to hospitals ranked high or low on a measure of disadvantage, running from −1 to +1 with 0 meaning proportional; negative values mean the return is concentrated among the less disadvantaged. The draws carry uncertainty in the specification, the coefficients and the published scores, and assumptions the draws cannot carry (the rate curve, the revenue base, the scorecard) are varied as declared scenarios. The scoring machinery was checked against HACRP, whose rule is public, and reproduced 3,054 of its 3,055 penalty decisions from the published inputs; that check covers the scoring the simulation shares with HACRP, not the Q-HIP-style rule.
