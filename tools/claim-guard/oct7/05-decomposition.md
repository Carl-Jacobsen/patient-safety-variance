# 5. How much of the score public characteristics describe

Public hospital and community characteristics describe a small share of the variation in published PSI-90 across the 2,569 hospitals in the model frame. Figure 1 shows the decomposition. The community block is associated with an estimated 3.3% of the variation (95% interval 2.5% to 4.8%), hospital structure with 4.5% (3.5% to 6.8%), and the three blocks together with 8.8%, of which community accounts for 37.8% and structure for 51.0%. These shares carry no direction: they say how much of the spread in scores lines up with each block, not whether disadvantaged catchments go with better or worse scores, which is the subject of section 6. Structure's estimate is the larger of the two, but on the published score their intervals overlap, so the decomposition does not clearly rank hospital structure above community as a description of PSI-90.

The community share depends on what is being decomposed. On the four component indicators, which CMS publishes without composite smoothing, community accounts for 1.44% of the between-hospital variance, with an interval from −0.60% to 3.67% that includes zero, so under that target the analysis does not establish a positive community share at all. Weighting each hospital by its estimated reliability gives 3.6% (2.8% to 5.3%) against the published score's 3.3%, a different descriptive allocation rather than a correction, since the reliability behind the weights is an approximation. Read together, the three versions put the community share of PSI-90's variation somewhere between nothing and about one twentieth, and in every version its estimate sits below structure's (7.5% on the component indicators).

The 91.2% left unexplained (95% interval 87.9% to 92.6%) should be read narrowly. It is conditional on these linear covariates, the complete-case frame and squared-error loss, and unmeasured practices, measurement error in the score, relationships the models do not capture and omitted covariates can all sit inside it. It is not the share of harm unrelated to hospitals or communities, and it is not a measure of hospital effort, which the data do not observe. Every share is also specific to the model frame: the 232 hospitals left out are smaller and sit in more deprived catchments, and a decomposition that could include them might give the community block a different share.

For the fairness question, the decomposition sets a ceiling rather than a verdict. If community conditions account for at most a few percent of the variation in published PSI-90 within this sample, then adjusting the score for those conditions could change hospitals' scores only modestly on average, whatever the direction of the adjustment, although a hospital near a payment cut point could cross it on a small change. That ceiling is a statement about averages; whether an adjustment would help or hurt particular hospitals, and in which direction it would move them, depends on the associations that section 6 examines, and those turn out to be much less settled than the shares.
