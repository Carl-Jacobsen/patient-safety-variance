# 3. Data

Every hospital, covariate and outcome comes from a public file whose address, retrieval date and hash are recorded in the project's provenance table of 38 sources, and a single driver script regenerates every number in this report from those files. The sample is every hospital that Care Compare lists as an acute care hospital with a numeric PSI-90 for the reporting window of July 2022 through June 2024, which gives 2,801 hospitals after the 107 Veterans Affairs hospitals are set aside. Table 2 shows how the sample narrows to the 2,569 hospitals that every composite model is fitted and scored on.

Table 2. Inclusion flow, from the Care Compare file to the composite model frame.

| Step | Hospitals remaining | Removed |
|---|---|---|
| Care Compare provider file | 4,790 | |
| Numeric PSI-90 published | 2,908 | 1,882 |
| Veterans Affairs hospitals set aside | 2,801 | 107 |
| Hospital structure (Block A) complete | 2,744 | 57 |
| Community measures (Block B) complete at every geographic grain | 2,700 | 44 |
| Contract labor observed and in range | 2,569 | 131 |

The 232 hospitals left out of the model frame are smaller than the rest and sit in more deprived catchments, so the findings describe the 2,569 and not every rated hospital. Most of the loss is contract labor, which the cost reports leave blank or implausible for 158 hospitals (139 missing after recovery and 19 outside the admissible range). The models that impute it treat those values as missing at random, a working assumption the data cannot confirm. Shifting every imputed value by one standard deviation up or down moves the community share of variation at the best-fitting imputation model from its 5.12% by at most 0.03 percentage points, but choosing between the complete-case and imputed frames moves the models' reliance on community measures far more than any shift does, so the complete-case restriction, not the imputation, is the missing-data assumption that matters here.

The outcome is the published PSI-90 composite, which CMS does not report as observed events against expected events. It publishes a smoothed estimate, in which each hospital's rate is pulled toward the national mean in proportion to how little data it contributes. From the published intervals the project derives a reliability for each hospital, written λ (the share of a hospital's own deviation from the mean that survives into the published number), under a scalar approximation to CMS's method. The median is 0.462, and reliability tracks volume closely (it correlates at +0.93 with the logarithm of Medicare volume): hospitals in the lowest quarter of volume average 0.205, those in the highest 0.755. A second official PSI-90 for the same window appears in the FY 2026 HACRP file and differs from the Care Compare value by a mean of 0.00191, largely because the two come from different versions of CMS's PSI software (one for Medicare and Veterans Affairs hospitals, one for Medicare alone) whose parameters are estimated on different reference populations; the Care Compare value is the outcome throughout, and the HACRP value is used only to check the payment simulation against the penalties HACRP assessed.

The covariates fall into three blocks. Block A describes the hospital through 11 measures from the CMS Impact Files and cost reports (size, occupancy, teaching, case mix, volume, safety-net share, wage index and finances). Block B, the community block, holds the four CDC Social Vulnerability Index themes, the Area Deprivation Index (ADI), the Index of Concentration at the Extremes for income, the uninsured and limited-English shares, rurality, primary-care physician density and market concentration. Block C holds coding intensity, contract labor and the state tort environment. Two vintages are older than the outcome: the SVI describes neighborhoods at a midpoint 3.0 years before the outcome window's, and the tort database ends 2.5 years before it.

Each community measure is an average over the hospital's Medicare catchment, weighted by its discharges from each ZIP code in the CMS Hospital Service Area File and carried to census tracts by population. CMS suppresses every cell with fewer than 11 discharges, and 87.46% of the file's cells are suppressed, so the weights are computed over the observed cells, which still account for an estimated 83% of the median hospital's discharges. Filling the suppressed cells at a realistic size changes the catchment SVI themes little (r ≥ 0.986 on all four), although section 6 shows larger effects on individual hospitals. For 130 hospitals too little of the catchment is observed, and they take their own county's values, flagged.
