# 6. Which covariates the credible models need

Every credible model relies on community disadvantage: scrambling the ten disadvantage measures together raises the prediction error of even the least reliant of the 194 credible models by 8.5%, and of the median model by 14.1%. Those same models disagree about the size and even the direction of the disadvantage association, which runs from −0.37 to +0.88 standard deviations across them. The two findings are compatible, and together they are the report's main result on which covariates matter to the models: no credible model can do without information about the communities hospitals serve, but the data do not determine how that information relates to PSI-90.

## 6.1 Reliance on the blocks

Figure 4 shows model class reliance, the range of reliance across the Rashomon set, for each block and measure. For the community disadvantage block, reliance runs from 1.085 in the least reliant credible model to 1.230 in the most reliant, and all 194 models lie above 1. For hospital structure, reliance runs from 1.112 to 1.266, so the least reliant credible model needs structure somewhat more than it needs community. The two blocks are close, which agrees with the variance decomposition, where structure's share was slightly larger than community's.

The two bounds respond differently when the tolerance is widened. Under the metropolitan tolerance the community bound falls only slightly, to 1.079, and the structure bound to 1.111. Under the state tolerance, which admits models the other two tolerances exclude, the community bound falls to 1.000, because the wider set includes models with no disadvantage covariate at all, while the structure bound holds at 1.097. The claim that no credible model can ignore community disadvantage therefore stands at the hospital and metropolitan tolerances and fails at the state tolerance, and it is reported as depending on that choice, while the claim for structure holds at all three.

## 6.2 Reliance on single measures

No single disadvantage measure comes close to the block. The measure with the highest lower bound, the limited-English share, reaches 1.016, against the block's 1.085, and the rest fall between 0.999 and 1.006: SVI theme 3 (racial and ethnic minority status) 1.006, SVI theme 4 (housing type and transportation) 1.003, rurality 1.002, ADI 1.001 and ICE 1.001, with SVI theme 1 at 1.001, market concentration (reported beside the block rather than in it) at 1.001, SVI theme 2 and the uninsured share at 1.000, and primary-care density at 0.999, the one measure some credible model does not need at all. The upper bounds are wider, reaching 1.149 for ICE and 1.099 for limited English, so individual models can depend heavily on one measure, but no measure is needed by every model to anything like the degree the block is.

The explanation is the overlap among the ten disadvantage measures. Each measure's reliance, taken on its own, is small because its neighbors carry much of the same information, while scrambling all ten together removes information none of them can supply. Structure behaves differently: bed count alone has a lower bound of 1.097, nearly the block's 1.112, and a range up to 1.499; Medicare volume (1.012) and operating margin (1.016) are the only other structural covariates that every credible model needs by more than 1%. The credible models' reliance on structure is concentrated in hospital size, while their reliance on community is spread across overlapping measures, none of which can be singled out by reliance alone.

## 6.3 Direction, where the models agree

For three disadvantage measures, every credible model agrees on the sign of the association (Figure 4, panel C). Moving from the 10th to the 90th percentile of rurality is associated with a PSI-90 higher by between 0.09 and 0.85 standard deviations, and the same move on SVI theme 3 with a PSI-90 higher by between 0.08 and 0.58, holding the other measures fixed. The limited-English share runs the other way in every credible model, between −0.99 and −0.08. Each of these is a conditional association, estimated with the other nine measures held constant, so the limited-English result says that among hospitals whose catchments are alike on the other measures, those serving more limited-English residents have lower PSI-90; it does not say that such hospitals have lower PSI-90 overall. The other seven measures straddle zero, and ICE has the widest range of all, from −0.45 to +1.30.

The Block C covariates enter as controls and carry no reliance bound, by a rule fixed before the analysis. Their ranges are reported so that the rival explanations remain visible. Contract labor share is associated with a higher PSI-90 in every credible model: a share one standard deviation higher goes with a PSI-90 higher by between 0.05 and 0.16 standard deviations, which agrees with the baseline model fitted before the specification analysis. Case mix drift, the partial proxy for coding intensity, and both tort measures straddle zero. Adding Block C moves the disadvantage estimate little (the median across specifications is +0.12 without Block C and +0.10 with it), but the partial proxy cannot rule out the coding explanation either, and the report does not claim it does.

## 6.4 Direct and shared importance

Figure 5 separates each measure's reliance into a direct part, which no combination of the other measures can reconstruct, and a shared part that rests on information correlated neighbors also carry. Summed over the ten measures, the median credible model's reliance beyond 1 is 0.133, of which 0.021, or 15.5%, is shared. Most of the importance is therefore the measures' own, but the sharing is concentrated in a few measures, and for those measures it changes how their importance should be read.

ADI is the clearest case of importance that is mostly shared. The other nine measures reconstruct 57% of its variation, and 78.5% of its reliance in the median credible model is shared with them, mainly with ICE and rurality. Its direct reliance stays above 1 in every credible model, so it carries some information of its own, but most of what makes ADI look important in a model is information the other measures also supply. SVI theme 3 is the reverse case: its ordinary reliance has a median of 0.998, which would suggest a model could drop it, but its direct reliance is above 1 in every credible model: the limited-English share offsets part of its contribution (the largest single flow in the table, −0.016), and once that is accounted for, SVI theme 3 carries information no other measure supplies. Summed over the flow table, ICE is the source of the most shared importance in other measures (+0.013), and rurality's reliance includes the most shared importance from others (+0.019).

The total importance score credits each measure with its direct reliance and with the part of other measures' shared importance that comes from it. Ranked by the median over the credible models, the order is SVI theme 3 (1.028), ICE (1.024), limited English (1.023), the uninsured share (1.021), rurality (1.016), ADI (1.013), SVI theme 1 (1.009), SVI theme 4 (1.009), SVI theme 2 (1.008) and primary-care density (1.005). The ranges across the set are wide and overlap (ADI's runs from 1.002 to 1.071), so neighboring ranks are not firmly separated. Seven of the ten measures have direct reliance above 1 in every credible model. The three that do not (the uninsured share, SVI theme 1 and primary-care density) reach their rank partly through other measures' shared importance that comes from them, and at least one credible model can do without their direct contribution.

A more familiar way of dividing importance, Shapley shares of explained variance, ranks the measures differently (a rank correlation of only +0.27 with the total importance score). The two answer different questions: Shapley divides the variance in PSI-90 that a model explains, and credits each measure with its average contribution; the total importance score divides the models' reliance, and credits a measure for the shared importance that comes from it as well as for its own direct reliance. ICE shows the difference most sharply: it ranks last on Shapley shares, contributing the least explained variance on average, and second on the total importance score, because much of its neighbors' shared importance comes from it.

## 6.5 A ranked minimum-viable dataset

Model 2 of the CASH framework asks for each input to be tagged essential, useful, substitutable or optional. Table 6.1 applies those tags to the ten disadvantage measures by a rule set on 2026-09-29, after the results were seen; the rule was not fixed in advance, and the tags should be read as a structured summary of the evidence rather than a pre-registered test. A measure is essential if its direct reliance is above 1 in every credible model and less than half of its reliance is shared; substitutable if its direct reliance is above 1 everywhere but half or more is shared; useful if its direct reliance does not clear 1 everywhere but its total importance score does; and optional otherwise.

Three qualifications apply to the tags in the table. SVI theme 2 clears the essential threshold only beyond the sixth decimal place, and a small change in the data could move it to useful. ICE is tagged substitutable because most of its own reliance is shared, yet it ranks second overall because much of other measures' shared importance comes from it; it is substitutable in the sense that neighbors could supply its signal, not in the sense that it could be dropped without loss. And every tag describes the models, not the world: an essential measure is one that no credible model on these data can replace with the others, not one that matters causally to patient safety.

With those qualifications, the table has a direct implication for data prioritization. The four essential measures are three of the four SVI themes and the limited-English share, all from the CDC Social Vulnerability Index file, while ADI, a measure often used on its own, is substitutable. A minimal dataset that kept ADI and dropped the SVI themes would keep a measure whose importance is mostly shared and discard three of the four that no credible model can replace. For CASH's purposes the better-supported choice is the SVI file with its themes kept separate, with ADI, ICE and rurality treated as largely interchangeable with one another.
