# 9. Limitations

Every finding in this report is bounded by what public hospital data can show, and several of the bounds can be measured. Each limitation is stated with its size wherever the project measured one, and with the direction in which it is likely to move the findings. The limitations fall into four groups: what the outcome measures, what the community covariates measure, what a cross-sectional ecological design can claim, and what the analytic and simulation choices leave open.

## 9.1 The outcome is smoothed, and unevenly

The published PSI-90 composite retains only part of each hospital's own deviation from the national mean, because CMS pulls each hospital's rate toward the mean in proportion to how little data it contributes. The median hospital retains 46% of its deviation (a reliability λ of 0.462). The spread is wide: a twentieth of hospitals fall below 0.14 and a twentieth above 0.84, with the middle half between 0.29 and 0.66. Hospitals in the lowest quarter of Medicare volume retain a mean of 0.205, those in the highest quarter 0.755, and across hospitals reliability correlates at +0.93 with the logarithm of Medicare volume. The composite therefore shrinks differences between hospitals most where hospitals are small.

The direction of that distortion matters for the equity findings, and it is not uniform across kinds of disadvantage. Reliability is nearly unrelated to a hospital's disproportionate share percentage (a correlation of +0.07) and to its Medicaid day share (+0.08), but it falls sharply with rurality: rural hospitals retain a mean of 0.304 against 0.520 for other hospitals, a correlation of −0.40 with the rural indicator. Two consequences follow from that pattern, one for urban and one for rural disadvantage. First, because reliability barely varies with payer mix, the smoothing pulls the association between PSI-90 and urban, payer-mix-related disadvantage toward zero without changing its pattern, so the urban results are conservative. Second, the rural association is the one the composite understates most, since the smoothing pulls rural hospitals toward the national mean more than any other group; a null or weak rural result on the published composite should not be read as evidence that rurality is unrelated to safety. The errors-in-variables specifications reverse the smoothing, and they raise the disadvantage estimate in 91.5% of paired comparisons, which is what this account predicts.

The reversal is only as good as the reliability estimates. CMS publishes neither the observed nor the expected rates behind the composite, so λ is recovered from the published confidence intervals, which are rounded to two decimal places. The recovery assumes that CMS's intervals are the posterior intervals of a normal smoothing model, which CMS does not document fully. The component measures avoid composite smoothing but lose coverage instead: PSI-13 is published for 2,313 of the 2,801 hospitals, and the hospitals missing it are smaller and more rural.

## 9.2 The catchments are Medicare catchments, partly observed

Every community covariate is an average over the census tracts a hospital's patients come from, weighted by the hospital's Medicare discharges from each ZIP code. The weights therefore describe the communities of Medicare fee-for-service patients, who are older than a hospital's overall patient population and include no one covered only by Medicaid or commercial insurance. Where a hospital's commercial or Medicaid patients come from different neighborhoods than its Medicare patients, its catchment covariates describe the wrong people to some degree. The size of that error cannot be measured from public data.

CMS suppresses every cell in the Service Area File with fewer than 11 discharges, and 87.46% of cells are suppressed. The weights are computed over the observed cells, which cover an estimated 83% of the median hospital's discharges. The measured cost is small: filling each suppressed cell at a realistic size changes the catchment-weighted SVI themes very little (r ≥ 0.986 on all four), and the tract-grain covariates agree with a suppression-free construction at 0.82 or higher. That test was run at county grain on 2,730 hospitals, and suppression was not tested separately at tract grain. For 130 hospitals (4.64%) too little of the catchment is observed, and they take their own county's values instead.

Three covariates carry construction limits of their own, beyond those of the catchment. Market concentration is computed from the same suppressed file, and suppression removes small competitors from the count, so it overstates concentration. The catchment ADI is a population-weighted average of block-group ranks rather than an ADI in the Neighborhood Atlas's own sense. And the 25 Connecticut hospitals take the statewide primary-care physician density, because the physician file uses Connecticut's old counties and could not be mapped to the new planning regions.

## 9.3 The covariates are older than the outcome

The Social Vulnerability Index describes neighborhoods at a data midpoint of mid-2020, and the PSI-90 window's midpoint is mid-2023, a gap of 3.0 years. That gap spans the economic disruption of the pandemic, when neighborhood employment, income and insurance coverage moved quickly and unevenly, so the SVI themes describe catchments as they were rather than as they were during the outcome window. The 2024 release of ADI and the ICE values from the 2020–2024 American Community Survey are more recent. The tort reform database ends in 2021, 2.5 years before the middle of the outcome window, and the tort measures should be read as the legal environment shortly before the period rather than during it.

## 9.4 Coding intensity is only partly addressed

The strongest rival explanation for an association between community disadvantage and worse PSI-90 is that it reflects documentation rather than safety: hospitals serving disadvantaged communities may code complications more thoroughly, or their patients may be sicker than risk adjustment captures. The report enters case mix index level and its year-on-year drift as a proxy for coding intensity, and that proxy is partial. It cannot distinguish more thorough documentation from sicker patients, it measures coding across all conditions rather than for the complications PSI-90 counts, and the measures that would address the objection directly (secondary diagnoses per discharge and present-on-admission completeness) require discharge-level data this project does not use. Case mix drift varies meaningfully across hospitals (a standard deviation of 0.0527) and its association with PSI-90 straddles zero in every credible model. The report does not claim that the coding and surveillance problem is solved. The community associations it reports could still partly reflect differences in how complications are documented.

## 9.5 What an ecological cross-section can claim

The design has two limits that no amount of data within it can remove. It is ecological: the covariates describe communities, and the outcome describes hospitals, so no finding in this report is a statement about individual patients. That catchments with higher deprivation are associated with worse hospital PSI-90 does not mean that deprived patients receive less safe care, or that any given patient's risk depends on their neighborhood; the associations could arise from differences in hospital resources, patient mix or documentation that happen to track community characteristics. The design is also cross-sectional, covering one two-year reporting window (July 2022 through June 2024). It cannot show whether a hospital's position changes when its community does, and it supports only associational language, which the report uses throughout.

## 9.6 Missing data and the modeling frame

Every variable with substantive missingness is missing disproportionately at small hospitals, and most serve more disadvantaged catchments. Multiple imputation, which was licensed for all of them, is used only for contract labor share (missing for 139 hospitals, 5.0%, after recovery from raw cost reports), and hospitals missing operating margin, uncompensated care or market concentration are left out of the models that use them. The component outcomes are never imputed, and only 2,294 hospitals report all four. The composite models with all three covariate blocks therefore run on 2,569 of the 2,801 hospitals, and the 232 left out are disproportionately small and serve more deprived catchments, which may understate associations concentrated among them. The imputations were drawn once and carried through the bootstrap rather than redrawn in each resample, which understates imputation uncertainty slightly. Ownership and system membership were assembled but never entered the models, so the structure block omits them.

## 9.7 What the model-multiplicity methods leave open

Four limits attach to the Rashomon and reliance results. First, the reliance bounds are computed over the 736 composite specifications the analysis listed, and a richer set of models could contain one that relies on a covariate less; the lower bounds are therefore upper bounds on what a larger search would find. Second, the conclusion that no credible model can ignore community disadvantage holds at the hospital and metropolitan tolerances and not at the state tolerance (9.3% of the best model's error), under which the reliance lower bound falls to 1.000; the choice between those tolerances turns on how independent hospitals within a state are, which the data cannot settle. Third, the direction of the disadvantage association is not settled: the credible models' estimates run from −0.37 to +0.88 standard deviations, and on the component measures community's share of between-hospital variation is not distinguishable from zero (1.5%, with an interval of −0.6% to 3.7%). Fourth, the Model 2 tags follow a rule chosen after the results were seen, and SVI theme 2's essential tag rests on a margin in the sixth decimal place. Separately from all four, about 91% of the variation in the composite is associated with none of the covariate blocks.

## 9.8 The archetype test

The Track test uses the CMS overall star rating in place of Leapfrog grades, which are sold rather than published. The star rating covers mortality, readmission, patient experience and timeliness as well as safety, and reading its bands as Leapfrog grades is an inference; the PSI-90 version of the test reaches the same verdict. The 208 hospitals without a star rating (7.4% of the sample) are small and fall outside the test. Safety-net status is proxied by disproportionate share percentage, which the memo never defines.

## 9.9 The payment simulation

The payment analysis is a national what-if built on the CASH2 mechanics, not an estimate of what Q-HIP pays, because Q-HIP's actual payments and contract terms are not public. Four of its inputs carry known limits, and two of its results carry qualifications. Commercial share is each hospital's non-public share of inpatient days, not of revenue; commercial payers pay more per day, so every dollar level runs low, although rankings and concentration indices are unaffected by a common factor. That share also counts self-pay and other payers as commercial, which overstates commercial share most where uninsurance is highest, at safety-net hospitals; the error works against finding the formula structurally regressive, so the ADI result is conservative as a statement about the formula's arithmetic. Sixteen hospitals without usable cost-report data take CASH2's default share. The infection measures in the safety score are held at their observed values rather than simulated. The check against HACRP also fell outside its preset bar in one place, the calibration slope among the least reliable quarter of hospitals (1.28 against 0.8 to 1.2), so the simulation is less decisive about small hospitals than their observed penalties were. Finally, the ADI result has no interval, because neither ADI nor a hospital's incentive per revenue dollar varies across draws; the absence of an interval reflects the absence of modeled uncertainty, not certainty, and it leaves out sampling error in the cost-report payer days the commercial share is built from.
