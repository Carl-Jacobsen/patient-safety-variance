# 7. Payment consequences

The fairness question already applies to a program in force. In FY 2026, HACRP reduced Medicare payments by 1% for the quarter of hospitals with the worst scores, and among the 2,551 hospitals in this analysis with a HACRP decision, those in the most deprived fifth of catchments were penalized at 1.39 times the rate of the least deprived fifth (95% interval 1.14 to 1.71, a normal approximation on the log scale); by the models' ranking of catchment disadvantage the ratio is 1.65 (1.35 to 2.02). Safety-net hospitals were penalized at 1.14 times the rate of others (0.98 to 1.33), an interval that includes parity. The intervals treat groups as fixed and hospitals as independent, and do not carry the model ranking's own uncertainty. HACRP's score combines PSI-90 with five infection measures, so these ratios describe the program's penalties in this frame, not PSI-90 alone, and not why.

Q-HIP's formula is not public at the detail a simulation needs, so the rest of this section applies a project-defined rank-based rule that uses CASH2's bonus band and revenue assumptions: a bonus of 0.1% to 4% of commercial revenue, set by a hospital's rank on a safety score in which PSI-90 carries 15% of the weight beside the HACRP infection measures. It is neither CASH2's own improvement formula nor an estimate of Q-HIP payments. Commercial revenue is net patient revenue times the share of inpatient days not paid by Medicare, Medicaid or Title V, a proxy that counts self-pay as commercial, treats days as revenue, and cannot tell which commercial dollars fall under an eligible contract. Every finding below describes this rule for the 2,569 hospitals of the common evaluation frame, and the dollar totals are national what-ifs. The simulation's scoring reproduces HACRP closely (627 penalties predicted against 629 observed, an area under the curve of 96.7%), a check of the scoring the two rules share, not of the rate curve, the revenue base or the score draws.

The quantity that matters for an incentive is the finite payment return: the change in a hospital's bonus if its PSI-90 improves by 0.05 while every other hospital's score and the scoring reference stay where they are, divided by the 0.05 step. If every hospital improves by 0.05 at once and the scoring reference (winsorization bounds, mean and standard deviation) is recalculated, every score, rank and bonus is unchanged with revenues fixed, and total payment changes by $0; holding the original reference instead moves 2,384 of the 2,569 hospitals' positions and total payment by −$56.5 million, a diagnostic rather than a prediction. The rule as simulated does not increase its payout for a common improvement. Attainment targets, fixed benchmarks or an explicit credit for year-over-year improvement, which Elevance describes, would reward collective progress; none was simulated.

Per dollar of revenue, the return falls more to hospitals in less deprived catchments, by an amount that depends on the scores' treatment. With every published score held at its value, the concentration index along catchment ADI is −0.035 and safety-net hospitals' return is 0.64 times other hospitals', with no interval, since nothing they depend on varies. The base scenario draws each score as a hypothetical repeated measurement under the reliability approximation, centered on the national mean plus λ times the hospital's published deviation; there the index is −0.058 (95% simulation interval −0.073 to −0.044) and the ratio 0.62 (0.59 to 0.65), intervals that reflect that draw alone. The direction is the same; the size rests on an unvalidated approximation. Under the base scenario a hospital in the most deprived fifth gains 39.23 basis points of revenue per full unit of PSI-90 improvement against 47.55 in the least deprived fifth, about 1.96 and 2.38 for the 0.05 step. Figure 3 shows these indices beside the observed HACRP penalty rates.

Much of the difference lines up with the revenue base. The estimated commercial share averages 0.238 in the most deprived fifth against 0.272 in the least, and 0.212 at safety-net hospitals against 0.274 at others, and per commercial dollar the gap nearly closes (1.621% against 1.680%). That attribution is only as good as the day-share proxy.

Table 4 shows how far the result depends on the assumptions. The ADI index stays negative with an interval that excludes zero under every rate curve, PSI-90 weight and commercial price ratio, weakens under CASH2's flat commercial share and a PSI-90-only scorecard, and shows no clear direction under CASH2's 0.40 safety-net revenue factor. The safety-net ratio stays below 1 in every scenario. Per hospital rather than per revenue dollar, safety-net hospitals' return is 1.18 times others', mostly because they are larger.

Along the pre-registered primary ranking, catchment disadvantage as the models score it, the result is on the edge under the score draws: the index is −0.038 (−0.085 to −0.0004), an endpoint within one Monte Carlo standard error of zero, and 8 of the 13 other scenarios show no clear direction, the fixed-score run among them (−0.017, −0.064 to +0.021). That is not a basis for calling the rule regressive along this ranking. Against distance from the best measured score the return shows no clear direction (−0.035 to +0.034), and only a convex rate curve concentrates it where that distance is small (−0.048, −0.086 to −0.009), so the rate curve's shape, a design choice, sets how much the hospitals furthest from the best score gain relative to others.

Under the base rule the simulated bonus totals $5.84 billion, and $3.62 billion to $9.05 billion across the scenarios, against eligible revenues that are not observed. For CASH, the observed HACRP ratios are the stronger evidence that a payment program scoring PSI-90 falls unevenly across communities today. The simulation adds that a bonus built on CASH2's band and revenue share would distribute its incentive unevenly too: among the assumptions varied, the revenue base moved that unevenness most, the scorecard moved it too, and the PSI-90 weight barely moved it; whether crediting improvement would change it is untested.
