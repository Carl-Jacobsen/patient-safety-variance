# 1. Summary and decision points

This report asks whether CMS's PSI-90, which HACRP penalizes on and a Q-HIP-style bonus would reward, follows the conditions of the communities hospitals serve. Public hospital and community characteristics describe a small share of its variation; community associations change across geographic constructions and model choices; and the payment scenarios identify assumptions that need validation before they can guide incentive design. The analysis observes neither safety effort nor how scores respond to investment, so each finding is an association across hospitals, and none separates a shortfall in risk adjustment from a real difference in care.

Across the 2,569 hospitals with every covariate observed, the community block is associated with 3.3% of the variation in published PSI-90 (95% bootstrap interval 2.5% to 4.8%) and hospital structure with 4.5% (3.5% to 6.8%), a difference the paired bootstrap does not settle. On the same hospitals, a model of the four component indicators gives community 1.62% of the between-hospital variance (bootstrap interval over hospital service areas, −0.81% to 4.19%). The 91.2% of published-composite variation left unexplained is conditional on these covariates and this sample, and it is not a measure of hospital effort. None of these shares says how far a community adjustment would move hospitals' scores or payments.

The direction of the association is unsettled. Every one of the 194 near-optimal models in the main set relies on the disadvantage measures at the primary tolerance (model class reliance of at least 1.085, a lower bound that falls to 1.000 at the state tolerance); a set calibrated on the published-score fits alone, which admits 105 models, keeps the bound above 1 at every tolerance. Yet the two sets' estimates of how predicted PSI-90 differs between catchments at the 10th and 90th percentiles of disadvantage range from −0.37 to +0.86 and from −0.23 to +0.35 standard deviations, ranges across models around a synthetic profile that lies farther from any observed catchment than all but 3.1% of observed catchments lie from their nearest neighbor. The answer also moves with how catchment measures are built, and filling suppressed discharge counts reorders individual hospitals' community contributions (rank correlation 0.840 among the 251 hospitals with the fewest mapped discharges), so public data, as built here, do not support a hospital-by-hospital adjustment.

Within settings chosen beforehand on all hospitals, the community measures earn a place in held-out states: dropping them raises held-out error by 2.2% (1.0% to 3.1% over 20 repeated fold assignments), the four SVI themes with the limited-English share are 0.37% worse than the full block (−0.98% to 1.34%), within the 1% tolerance chosen for the comparison, and ADI alone falls 1.30% short (0.11% to 2.25%). The base is small: the best model explains 4.0% of the variation in held-out states.

HACRP, which scores PSI-90 beside five infection measures, already penalizes unevenly: in FY 2026, among the 2,551 hospitals with a HACRP decision, those in the most deprived fifth of catchments were penalized at 1.39 times the rate of the least deprived fifth (1.14 to 1.71, a normal approximation on the log scale). The simulation applies a project-defined rank-based rule, using CASH2's bonus band and revenue assumptions, to the 2,569 hospitals. A common improvement, with the scoring reference recalculated, leaves its payout unchanged. Its return to improvement per revenue dollar falls with catchment deprivation (concentration index −0.035 with published scores held fixed; −0.058, simulation interval −0.073 to −0.044, with scores drawn under the unvalidated reliability approximation), much of it lining up with the commercial share of revenue, and loses its direction under CASH2's 0.40 safety-net revenue factor.

Four decisions follow for CASH. First, do not adjust PSI-90 for community conditions hospital by hospital on public data alone. Second, if CASH keeps a community-data module for safety-score analysis, start it from the SVI themes with the limited-English share rather than ADI alone, as a candidate supported by this conditional validation, not a minimum dataset for a CASH2 business case. Third, judge any incentive design on its revenue base, which moved the concentration more than any other assumption varied, on the eligible revenue that base stands in for, and on whether it rewards collective improvement, which this rule does not and this simulation did not model. Fourth, choose the next question, fairness with patient-level data or return on investment with hospital-level inputs, knowing that the CASH2 Tracks do not correspond to stable groups in the national file.
