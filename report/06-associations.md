# 6. Community associations across models and geography

At the primary tolerance the near-optimal models agree that the community measures carry information about PSI-90, and they disagree about what that information says. Figure 2 shows the reliance bounds. At the primary tolerance, scrambling the ten disadvantage measures raises the prediction error of every one of the 194 models in the main Rashomon set (MCR⁻ 1.085, MCR⁺ 1.230), a lower bound that holds at the metropolitan-area tolerance (1.079 across 319 models) and fails at the state tolerance, where the set admits models without the community block and the bound falls to 1.000. That set mixes weighted, errors-in-variables and unweighted fits and is chosen through a best fit that depends on the reliability approximation. The published-score set, calibrated on the unweighted fits alone, admits 105 models and gives lower bounds of 1.079, 1.063 and 1.048 at its primary, metropolitan-area and state tolerances, so on the published score the bound holds at all three. The bounds cover only the enumerated specifications; a richer class of models could contain one that relies on the community measures less.

On direction the models do not agree. Moving every disadvantage measure from its 10th to its 90th percentile changes predicted PSI-90 by −0.37 to +0.86 standard deviations across the 194 models, with 57.2% of them positive (worse scores in more disadvantaged catchments), and by −0.23 to +0.35 across the published-score set. In its best model (specification 382, county catchment) and that model's siblings at the other three grains, the contrast is −0.21 (standard error 0.20) at the county catchment, +0.13 (0.19) at the tract catchment, −0.04 (0.19) at the hospital's own county and +0.09 (0.18) at the hospital service area, none separated from zero; the service-area model is fitted to 2,605 hospitals and the others to 2,569, so that comparison also changes the sample. The joint move is a synthetic profile. Between 12% and 34% of observed catchments sit at least as far from the center as its endpoints, which passes the rule fixed in advance, but that does not show observed catchments lie near them: at the county catchment the most disadvantaged endpoint is 2.07 from its nearest observed catchment, a distance only 3.1% of observed catchments reach from their own nearest neighbor. The contrast is a model-based scenario, not a comparison between existing places.

In those four models the disadvantage measures do not move together: the part of the contrast carried by demographic composition (SVI 2, SVI 3 and the limited-English share) is negative at every grain (−0.15 to −0.29), the part carried by rurality and primary-care density is positive (+0.16 to +0.24), and the part carried by material deprivation (ADI, the income index, SVI 1, SVI 4 and the uninsured share) changes sign with the grain. A negative coefficient on the limited-English share does not mean limited-English populations are protected, nor a positive one on the minority-status theme a patient-level risk; both associate catchment averages with a hospital score, conditional on the rest of the model. A supplementary comparison, chosen after seeing the data, averages fitted predictions over the observed community profiles in each extreme decile, other inputs fixed: in the county-catchment model the predicted contrasts are +0.23 standard deviations for material deprivation (model-based standard error 0.10) and −0.44 for composition (0.11), with the same signs at every grain. The raw differences in mean published PSI-90 between the same deciles are +0.15 and −0.22.

How the catchment measures are built changes the answer. The SVI themes are ranks computed separately for tracts and for counties; rebuilding them from comparable tract quantities moves the county-catchment contrast from −0.21 to −0.19 standard deviations and the tract-catchment contrast from +0.13 to approximately 0.00; the gap, for which no interval was computed, falls from 0.34 to 0.19. In the term accounting the rest is carried mainly by the fitted income-index and limited-English terms, which differ by how they aggregate; that says where the gap sits, not why. Adding ownership and system membership moves the contrast from −0.21 to −0.24 at the best model and from +0.14 to +0.10 at the median one.

The suppressed discharge counts matter little for the averages and a great deal for individual hospitals. Filling every suppressed cell at 10 discharges changes the best county-grain model's error by 0.12% and its contrast by 0.044, but the rank correlation of hospitals' community contributions falls to 0.924, and to 0.840 among the 251 hospitals with the fewest mapped discharges, where one hospital moves 1,696 places of 2,569. These scenarios carry no probability; they show that public data built this way do not support a hospital-by-hospital community adjustment of PSI-90.

The held-out validation (Table 3) refits settings chosen using every hospital's outcome, so it compares datasets within those settings, not the selection. In held-out states, dropping the community block raises error by 2.2% (1.0% to 3.1% over 20 repeats). The four SVI themes with the limited-English share are 0.37% worse than the full block (−0.98% to 1.34%), within the 1% mean-loss tolerance chosen for this comparison and within 1% in 90% of repeats, a practical rule rather than a statistical equivalence test; ADI alone falls 1.30% short (0.11% to 2.25%). Within known areas the SVI set keeps 83% of the full block's gain (73% to 97%) and ADI alone 44% (33% to 53%). The base is small: the best model explains 4.0% of the variation in held-out states and 6.8% within known areas, against 11.0% in sample.

For the fairness question, the data do not establish that PSI-90 penalizes hospitals in disadvantaged catchments, nor that it favors them; the sign depends on which measures carry it, at what grain they are built, and which of several equally good models one reads. What they support is narrower: within the selected settings, a model that leaves out the community measures predicts worse in held-out states, and the SVI themes with the limited-English share are a better candidate module than ADI alone.
