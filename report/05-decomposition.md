# 5. How much of the score public characteristics describe

Public hospital and community characteristics describe a small share of the variation in published PSI-90 across the 2,569 hospitals of the common evaluation frame. Figure 1 shows the decomposition. The community block is associated with an estimated 3.3% of the variation (95% interval 2.5% to 4.8%), hospital structure with 4.5% (3.5% to 6.8%), and the three blocks together with 8.8%, of which community accounts for 37.8% and structure for 51.0%. These shares carry no direction: they say how much of the spread in scores lines up with each block, not whether disadvantaged catchments go with better or worse scores, which is the subject of section 6. Structure's estimate is the larger of the two, but the difference was tested directly from the same bootstrap replicates, and structure minus community is +1.2 percentage points with a paired 95% interval of −0.4 to +3.4, so the decomposition does not clearly rank hospital structure above community as a description of PSI-90.

The community share depends on what is being decomposed. On the four component indicators, which CMS publishes without composite smoothing, and fitted to the same 2,569 hospitals, community accounts for 1.62% of the between-hospital variance, with an interval from −0.81% to 4.19%, an interval that includes zero, so under that target the analysis does not establish a positive community share. On the same target structure accounts for 6.1%, and the paired difference, +4.5 percentage points (1.0 to 10.3), does rank structure above community. The October 7 draft's figure for this share, and its interval, came from subset models that each dropped only the rows missing their own terms, so its eight models were fitted to different hospitals; both are withdrawn. Weighting each hospital by its estimated reliability gives 3.6% (2.8% to 5.3%) against the published score's 3.3%, a different descriptive allocation rather than a correction, since the reliability behind the weights is an approximation. The three versions use different weights, outcomes and variance targets, so they are not three estimates of one quantity.

The 91.2% left unexplained (95% interval 87.9% to 92.6%) should be read narrowly. It is conditional on these linear covariates, the complete-case frame and squared-error loss, and unmeasured practices, measurement error in the score, relationships the models do not capture and omitted covariates can all sit inside it. It is not the share of harm unrelated to hospitals or communities, and it is not a measure of hospital effort, which the data do not observe. Every share is also specific to the frame: the 232 hospitals left out are smaller and sit in more deprived catchments, and a decomposition that could include them might give the community block a different share.

For the fairness question, the measured community block receives a small share of the total variation in this linear decomposition. That share does not say how far a community adjustment would move hospitals' scores or payment decisions. A Shapley share is an average gain over orders of entry, and the same fit answers neighboring questions differently: the community block alone describes 3.6% of the variation, it adds 3.1% when it enters last, and its fitted term has a variance equal to 6.1% of the variation in PSI-90 (a standard deviation of 0.25 of the score's). None of these is an adjustment. How far a particular adjustment would move scores, and which hospitals would cross a payment cut point, would have to be computed for that adjustment, which this report did not do; which hospitals it would help or hurt depends on the associations section 6 examines, which are much less settled than the shares.
