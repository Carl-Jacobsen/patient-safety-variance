# 4. Methods

Many models fit these data about equally well without giving the same answer about community conditions, so the methods report the range of answers among well-fitting models rather than the answer of one preferred model. The outcome throughout is the published PSI-90 score, the number CMS reports and pays on. Some analyses also use a reliability for each hospital, derived under a scalar approximation to CMS's smoothing whose accuracy for this release is not known: it weights hospitals in sensitivity fits, reverses the smoothing in the errors-in-variables fits, helps choose the main set of near-optimal models and shapes the payment simulation's score draws. Each result that depends on it is reported beside a version that does not.

The first method divides the variation in PSI-90 among the three covariate blocks, averaging each block's incremental contribution over all orders of entry (LMG or Shapley averaging), so the allocation does not depend on choosing one order, with intervals from 1,000 resampled versions of the data. The same decomposition is run on the four component indicators, which CMS publishes without composite smoothing, with every subset model fitted to the same 2,569 hospitals.

The second method maps the choices an analyst could reasonably make (blocks, functional form, outcome, reliability treatment, geographic grain, missing values, unusual hospitals and scale). Every combination was registered before any model was fitted, which gives 3,496 specifications, 736 of them for the composite score. Each specification that includes the community block yields a contrast: the difference in predicted PSI-90, in standard deviations, between a catchment at the 10th percentile of every disadvantage measure and one at the 90th.

The third method narrows those specifications to the ones that fit nearly as well as the best, a Rashomon set. A model belongs to the set if its prediction error exceeds the best model's by no more than a tolerance, set by procedure fixed in writing beforehand: the 95th percentile of the excess error of the best model refitted on 1,000 resampled versions of the data. Resampling hospitals, the primary unit, gives 4.1% of the best model's error and admits 194 of the 736 composite specifications; resampling metropolitan areas gives 5.1%, and states 9.3%. The best model is an errors-in-variables fit, so this main set is chosen partly through the reliability approximation. After the October 7 audit the procedure was repeated on the 184 composite specifications fitted to the published score without weights: their best fit, specification 382, and its own tolerance (2.9% of its error) admit 105, the published-score set. Every model is scored on the same 2,569 hospitals, the common evaluation frame, but each is fitted to the hospitals complete on its own terms, from 803 to 2,744 of them.

The fourth method measures how much the models depend on the community measures. A model's reliance on a group of covariates is the ratio of its prediction error after their values are scrambled across hospitals to its error before, so 1 means no reliance. Model class reliance (MCR) reports the lowest and highest reliance across the set. A lowest value above 1 says that none of the enumerated near-optimal models can ignore the group without losing accuracy; it says nothing about direction, describes the models rather than the world, and holds only for the 736 specifications listed and the tolerance stated.

The fifth method refits specification 382's settings with four versions of the community block (none, all of it, ADI alone, and the four SVI themes with the limited-English share) and scores each on held-out hospitals, in folds of whole states and in folds of hospitals within known areas, each repeated over 20 random fold assignments. A reduced version meets the chosen tolerance if its mean held-out error is within 1% of the full block's, a practical rule rather than a statistical equivalence test. Specification 382 was itself chosen using every hospital's outcome, so the validation compares candidate datasets within those settings and does not test the selection. It was added after the external review.

The sixth method simulates payment under a project-defined rank-based rule that uses CASH2's bonus band and revenue assumptions, described in section 7. The quantity of interest is the finite payment return, the change in a hospital's bonus if its PSI-90 improves by a fixed amount while every other hospital's score stays put, divided by that amount and by the hospital's revenue. Its concentration index, from −1 to +1, summarizes whether the return falls more to hospitals ranked high or low on a measure of disadvantage; negative values mean it is concentrated among the less disadvantaged. The draws carry uncertainty in the specification and the coefficients and draw each published score as a hypothetical repeated measurement under the reliability approximation, so every payment result is also reported with the published scores held fixed. The rate curve, revenue base and scorecard are varied as declared scenarios. The scoring machinery reproduced 3,054 of HACRP's 3,055 penalty decisions from the published inputs, a check of the scoring the two rules share, not of the Q-HIP-style rule.
