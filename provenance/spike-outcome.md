## 5. Observed vs expected: **there is no O/E split, and none can be reconstructed**

> **SUPERSEDED IN PART, 2026-08-26.** This section understated the cost. The shrinkage
> is now quantified in `plan-amendments.md` §1: the published composite retains a
> **median 46%** of each hospital's true deviation (mean λ = 0.475), the retention is
> almost perfectly determined by volume (r = +0.93), and it is therefore **differential
> with respect to rurality (λ 0.304 rural vs 0.520 urban) but not with respect to DSH
> or Medicaid share**. A correction is available and is now in scope. Read §1 of
> `plan-amendments.md` with this section.

This is the substantive negative finding of the spike.

**What CMS publishes.** Per the FY 2026 fact sheet, the CMS PSI 90 composite value is
the *sum product of each component PSI's smoothed rate multiplied by its component
weight*, where the component weight is the product of a harm weight and a volume
weight. Each component's smoothed rate is a reliability-adjusted, risk-standardized
observed-to-expected ratio. The O/E ratio therefore exists inside the CMS calculation
but is not published at hospital level.

**What is published:**
- composite: one number per hospital, ratio scale, national mean ≈ 1.0
- components: one risk-adjusted rate per 1,000 per hospital, plus the at-risk denominator
- **not published:** observed numerator, expected rate, expected count, or the
  smoothing shrinkage factor

**Reconstruction is not possible from public files.** The component `Score` is already
risk-adjusted and smoothed; the raw observed numerator is nowhere in the public
release, and recovering it would require discharge-level data (HCUP path) plus the
CMS PSI software risk models.

### What the analysis does instead

The plan (§4) already anticipates this: the composite "applies reliability weighting
and smoothing that conceal heterogeneity the argument depends on, and the components
measure what that concealment costs." That design survives the absence of an O/E split
intact, but two things must change from a naive reading:

1. **The composite is not an outcome to be modeled as if unsmoothed.** Shrinkage is
   volume-dependent: low-volume hospitals are pulled harder toward the national mean.
   Since volume correlates with the Block A and Block B covariates the study cares
   about, shrinkage induces a *covariate-correlated attenuation* in the composite.
   This is a first-order threat and belongs in Limitations, not a footnote.
2. **The component analysis is the identification of that attenuation, not a
   robustness check.** Components are published as risk-adjusted rates with
   denominators attached, so the shrinkage is far weaker and the denominator is
   available as a precision weight. The composite-vs-component gap is the estimand
   for "what smoothing conceals." This promotes the component models from secondary
   to structural, and Phase 3's specification space must include *outcome definition*
   (composite vs each component vs a denominator-weighted component aggregate) as an
   enumerated axis — which plan §6 already lists.

Two mitigations are available and cheap, and should be carried into Phase 3:

- **Denominator-weighted estimation on the components**, using the published
  `Denominator` as a precision weight, recovers most of what an O/E split would buy
  for the component models.
- **CI width as a reliability proxy on the composite.** `Higher Estimate − Lower
  Estimate` for `PSI_90` is a published, monotone proxy for how hard a hospital was
  shrunk. It can enter as a specification-space axis (weight by inverse CI width) and
  as a diagnostic — if the community-disadvantage association strengthens among
  narrow-CI hospitals, smoothing is attenuating it.

## 6. Suppression and missingness

Suppression is **footnote-coded, not blank-coded**, and the codes distinguish
mechanisms that must not be collapsed. Decoded from `Footnote_Crosswalk.csv`:

| Code | Text | Mechanism | Handling |
|---|---|---|---|
| `1` | The number of cases/patients is too few to report | **volume suppression** | MNAR w.r.t. volume — never impute, model as a stratum or exclude with the count reported |
| `5` | Results are not available for this reporting period | not reported | missing |
| `7` | No cases met the criteria for this measure | **structural zero** — denominator is genuinely 0 | not missing; distinct from `1` |
| `13` | Results cannot be calculated for this reporting period | not calculable | missing |
| `19` | Data shown only for hospitals in IQR/OQR programs | **out of frame** | not a PSI-reporting hospital |
| `23` | Hospital reported discrepancies in its claims data | **quality flag on a present value** | value is present; flag it, sensitivity-test dropping these |
| `29` | Calculated using partial performance period data (CMS-approved exception) | **partial window** | value present; flag; candidate exclusion in the specification space |

Footnote counts across all PSI rows of the provider file: blank 31,112; `13` 16,385;
`1` 3,802; `19` 2,064; `5` 1,747; `7` 1,490; `29` 688; `23` 96; `1, 29` 95; `1, 23` 1.
Footnotes are **comma-concatenated multi-codes** — parse as a list, not a scalar.

Codes `23` and `29` attach to hospitals that *do* have a numeric value. They are the
raw material for two influential-point axes in the Phase 3 specification space.
For `PSI_90`: 16 hospitals carry `23`, 61 carry `29`, and 7 carry `1, 29`.

## 8. Cross-source validation: two published PSI-90 values, and they differ

The HACRP file and the Care Compare six-decimal file both publish a PSI-90 composite
for the identical 2022-07-01 to 2024-06-30 window. On the 2,793 hospitals present in
both with numeric values:

- Pearson r = **0.999606**
- mean difference (HACRP − Care Compare) = **+0.00191**
- SD of difference = 0.005411, 99th pct |diff| = 0.019169, max |diff| = 0.047078
- exact agreement to 5 decimal places: 38 of 2,793

They are not the same number. The most likely explanation is a different data
snapshot — the HACRP value is frozen at the FY 2026 review-and-correct deadline,
the Care Compare value reflects the July 2026 refresh — but this has not been
confirmed and should not be asserted in the report without confirmation.

**Consequence:** the analysis must pick one and say which. Recommendation:
**Care Compare six-decimal as the outcome** (it is the plan's named source, has
six-decimal precision, and covers 107 more hospitals), with **HACRP reserved for
Phase 4** as the payment anchor. The 0.002 mean offset is immaterial at that
separation of roles, but the report should state that two official values exist and
that they differ by this much, because a reviewer who checks against HACRP will
otherwise find a discrepancy and assume an error.
