# Task S3.4 — Correct the archetype account

**Date:** 2026-10-05 · **Budget:** 2 h · **Status: done. Stage 46,
post-review.** The Track verdict stands: a gradient, not strata. What was
wrong was the account of it.

- **Clustering.** The draft said the clustering "found no separated groups at
  all". The criteria disagree with each other. ICL selects 2 groups on the
  published score and 4 on the λ-corrected score, the gap statistic selects 1
  on both, and ICL's own choice recurs in only 44% and 59% of subsamples. What
  the analysis supports is narrower: it does not establish stable three-Track
  groups.
- **The Track cells.** The full nine-cell table shows 1,591 of 2,581 hospitals
  (61.6%) off the Track diagonal.
- **Alternative bands.** None of the three preregistered band designs reaches
  the 50% operational criterion. The widest middle bands come closest (48.9%),
  but almost all of that is concentration expected by chance (45.7%).
- **Beds.** The comparison is recast as different units and different kinds
  of number.

## 2. The cluster criteria, in one sentence

Generated by stage 46 from `cluster-summary.csv` and stage 33's log. For S5:

> On operating margin and PSI-90 (2,759 hospitals), the preregistered
> criterion, ICL, selects 2 groups on the published score and 4 on the
> λ-corrected score, while the gap statistic selects 1 on both; ICL's own
> choice recurs in 44% and 59% of 100 random 80% subsamples. The analysis
> therefore does not establish stable three-Track groups.

This sits apart from the Track cross-tabulation, which asks a different
question: not whether the data cluster, but whether hospitals concentrate in
the three cells the Tracks name. The adjusted Rand index of the forced
three-group solution against the Track cells (−0.003) belongs with the
clustering, not with the cross-tabulation's verdict.

## 4. Alternative bands (second-pass preregistration sec 7)

| Design | Diagonal share [95%] | Expected under independence | Observed / expected | γ [95%] | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| original (terciles; 1–2 / 3 / 4–5 stars) | 38.4% [36.5, 40.2] | 33.3% | 1.15 [1.10, 1.21] | +0.206 [+0.155, +0.253] | gradient, not strata |
| (i) margin quartiles (Q1 / Q2–Q3 / Q4) | 37.8% [36.1, 39.6] | 33.0% | 1.15 [1.10, 1.20] | +0.219 [+0.169, +0.271] | gradient, not strata |
| (ii) stars 1 / 2–4 / 5 | 37.3% [35.9, 38.6] | 33.3% | 1.12 [1.08, 1.16] | +0.319 [+0.232, +0.390] | gradient, not strata |
| (iii) both | 48.9% [47.8, 50.5] | 45.7% | 1.07 [1.04, 1.11] | +0.336 [+0.261, +0.418] | gradient, not strata |

**Reading.**
- The verdict holds under every preregistered design.
- Design (iii) comes within 1.1 points of the bar, and its interval reaches
  50.5%. But it does so because it puts 2,141 hospitals (83%) in the middle
  star band and half in the middle margin band, which raises the diagonal
  share expected by chance to 45.7%. Its ratio over independence (1.07) is the
  lowest of the four.
- So the 50% criterion is an operational rule. It answers "do most hospitals
  sit in a Track cell?" for a given banding, and band widths alone can move it
  most of the way to the bar. The ratio over independence and γ are what
  measure the association, and both show a modest positive gradient under
  every design.
- S5 should present 50% as an operational criterion fixed in advance, not as a
  property of the population.
